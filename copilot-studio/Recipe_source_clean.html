Library Enquiry Email Assistant

●●○  Estimated time: 25 minutes

(recipe from the Librarian Email Agent project, LITE Lab@HKU)

Purposes:

  • Triage an incoming library mailbox: answer routine enquiries from the library's own published FAQ, and route everything else to a person.
  • Keep every factual sentence traceable to an FAQ entry, so a wrong opening time or loan period cannot reach a student.
  • Return a structured decision rather than free prose, so the send-or-hold choice stays outside the model and can be audited.
  • Never auto-send a meeting request, a complaint, or mail in a language whose reply quality has not been checked.

Step-by-step guide:

1.

Navigate to https://copilotstudio.microsoft.com and sign in with your HKU account. Confirm the environment shown at the bottom left is connect.hku.hk.

Click Agents in the left sidebar, then create a new agent. You will land on the Build tab, which is where everything in this recipe happens.

The Build tab. Instructions on the left; Model, Knowledge and Tools on the right.

Worth doing before you fill anything in: save the empty agent and check it succeeds. Every field on this page works whether or not your account is licensed to create agents, so an access problem only surfaces at the moment you try to keep something.

2.

Click the agent name and rename it. For this recipe: HKU Library Enquiry Assistant

The name field silently truncates at 30 characters. "HKU Libraries Enquiry Assistant" is 31 and quietly loses its last letter, so check what you typed is what appears.

3.

Fill in the Instructions box with the prompt below, replacing the placeholder text.

Note what it asks the agent to produce: not a reply, but a structured decision. The flow around the agent reads ACTION and decides whether to send. Keeping the irreversible choice outside the model is the whole design.

You are the enquiry assistant for HKU Libraries. You read incoming email and either answer it from the library's published FAQ or hand it to a librarian. You never invent library information. STEP 1. Classify every email as exactly one of these five: enquiry: a question a library user wants answered meeting_request: someone wants to meet, book time, or arrange a consultation internal_admin: colleagues, logistics, forms, building notices automated_notification: system-generated mail that needs no reply needs_human: complaints, sensitive matters, anything unclear or off-topic If you are unsure between enquiry and needs_human, choose needs_human. Sending a wrong answer costs more than asking a person to look. STEP 2. Grounding rules. These override everything else. Use ONLY the FAQ knowledge provided to you. Do not use the web. Do not use general knowledge about libraries or about other universities. Never state a number, time, price, date, fee, loan period or policy that does not appear word for word in the FAQ text you are citing. Quote policy sentences from the FAQ rather than rephrasing them. Stiff and correct beats fluent and wrong. If the FAQ does not answer the question, say that a librarian will follow up. Do not guess, and do not partially answer from memory. Keep replies under 100 words. Sign off as HKU Libraries. STEP 3. Reply in exactly this format, every time: CATEGORY: one of the five above LANGUAGE: english, chinese, or other SOURCE: the exact FAQ question you used, or NONE ACTION: send, hold_for_review, or no_reply REPLY: the reply body, or NONE STEP 4. How to choose ACTION. Choose send ONLY when all four are true: (1) CATEGORY is enquiry, (2) LANGUAGE is english, (3) SOURCE is not NONE, and (4) every fact in REPLY appears word for word in that FAQ entry. Choose no_reply when CATEGORY is automated_notification. Choose hold_for_review in every other case. This includes meeting requests, where you must not propose, accept or decline a time; instead put a one sentence summary of who wants to meet and what about in REPLY. It also includes anything written in Chinese or another language, because reply quality in those languages has not been checked and a person must read it first. And it includes anything you are unsure about. When in doubt, choose hold_for_review. A held draft costs a librarian ten seconds. A wrong answer sent from the library's address costs a student a wasted trip, a missed deadline, or a fine they did not owe.

Type or paste as plain text. The Instructions box is a rich-text editor, so lines beginning with "-" or "#" may be turned into bullets and headings.

The Instructions box after filling it in.

4.

Remove a knowledge source before you add one. In the Knowledge panel on the right, click the × on the Search all websites chip. It is enabled by default.

This is the most important click in the recipe. An agent that can search the web will answer library policy questions from whatever it finds, which destroys the one property the design depends on: every factual sentence traceable to an HKU FAQ entry.

Then click + next to Knowledge and upload your FAQ document — the questions and answers copied verbatim from the library's own published FAQ, with source URLs.

The Add knowledge dialog. Upload the FAQ document rather than pointing at a website.

In settings, turn off "allow ungrounded responses" and any "use information from the web". Ungrounded responses let the model answer from general knowledge when the FAQ has no answer, which is exactly the failure this design exists to prevent.

5.

Open the Preview tab and paste in two or three real enquiries.

Two things to watch. Does the reply come back in the CATEGORY / LANGUAGE / SOURCE / ACTION / REPLY format? And when you ask about opening hours or fees, does it quote the FAQ, or does it produce a confident, fluent, invented answer? The second is the test that matters.

Then connect the mailbox: add a trigger for when a new email arrives in a shared mailbox, set it to split on the value property so it handles one message at a time, and point it at a test mailbox first — never the library's live inbox.

6.

When the agent behaves, save and publish it.

Publishing makes the agent available to the channels you configured; it does not by itself put it in front of library users. Keep the trigger pointed at a test mailbox until you have watched a real week of mail go through it and read what it drafted.

Check the send-or-hold split in the first few days. If almost everything is held, the FAQ probably does not cover what people actually ask, and the gap is the useful finding.

Practical Tips

  • A prompt instruction is not a control. In our own testing a small model invented library opening hours while the correct FAQ text sat in its context and the prompt said, in those words, never to invent opening hours. Step 2 of the prompt reduces invention; it does not prevent it. Anything that must not be wrong should be quoted verbatim by the surrounding flow, not written by the model.
  • Give the model the same FAQ your staff use, as a file. Uploading the document rather than pointing at a website keeps the source fixed, which is what lets you compare two builds fairly or explain later why a particular answer was given.
  • Have the flow, not the model, decide to send. Read the ACTION line, and consider having the flow verify that any number in the reply appears in the cited FAQ entry before it sends. If that check cannot be built, the honest setting is drafts-only.
  • Measure it before you trust it. Hand-label twenty real enquiries with the right category and the FAQ entry that should answer them, run them through, and count. "It seems to work" is not a result; "15 of 20, and every error sent mail toward a human rather than away from one" is.
  • Test against a test mailbox. A confidently wrong reply from the library's official address is an institutional problem, not a bug report.
